Ground Report · Ludhiana, Punjab

In a Ludhiana village, a retired teacher’s courtyard has become the evening school

No fees, no uniforms, no registers. When the fields go quiet, children walk to her home to learn.

HK
Harleen Kaur8 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

The first children arrive a little after five, their school bags still on their backs. By six, the courtyard is full — older students helping younger ones with sums, a blackboard propped against the wall.

She taught in government schools for most of her working life. When she retired, she says, the house felt too quiet.

A classroom without walls

There is no fee and no sign outside. Parents send vegetables, a sack of flour at harvest, sometimes a new box of chalk.

“Nobody asks them for marks here. They come because they want to learn.”

Teachers at the nearby government school have noticed the difference. Homework comes back finished. Children who barely spoke in class now raise their hands.
